# Diet-related health burden in Myanmar (for Sar AI)

Notes compiled 2026-11-01. **Big caveat that frames everything below: the latest nationally representative measured data for Myanmar are from the WHO STEPS survey of Sept–Dec 2014 (adults 25–64, n = 8,757, 52 townships).** No national NCD survey since then was found (the 2015–16 DHS measured women's BMI only). Since 2014 all numbers are model estimates (IDF, NCD-RisC/Global Nutrition Report) or small local studies. Treat 2014 figures as a floor: urban diets and weight have very likely moved since then.

## 1. Prevalence of overweight/obesity, diabetes, pre-diabetes and hypertension (with absolute numbers)

### Takeaway
In 2014 about **1 in 10 adults aged 25–64 had diabetes (10.5%)** and **1 in 5 had pre-diabetes (~20%)**. About **27% were overweight or obese by WHO cut-offs (20.4% overweight + 6.5% obese)**. Hypertension affected roughly **22–35% depending on survey and place** (about 30% nationally in 2009). IDF models **~2.4 million adults (20–79) with diabetes in 2024**, rising to **3.3 million by 2050**. At Asian BMI cut-offs, **41% of women aged 18–49 were overweight or obese in 2015–16**.

### Cited Findings
**Diabetes and pre-diabetes (STEPS 2014, measured)**
- National diabetes prevalence was 10.5% (men 9.1%, women 11.8%) among people aged 25–64 in Sept–Dec 2014; n = 8,757 (93.6% response). Diabetes was defined as FPG > 7.0 mmol/L and/or 2-h OGTT > 11.1 mmol/L or on medication, using capillary blood on a portable device. The paper's abstract gives slightly different numbers (10.8%; men 11.5%, women 9.2%), an internal inconsistency to flag — [Latt et al., DMSO 2019](https://www.dovepress.com/measurement-of-diabetes-prediabetes-and-their-associated-risk-factors--peer-reviewed-fulltext-article-DMSO); also at [PMC6407671](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6407671/)
- Pre-diabetes was 19.7–20% (men 16.5%, women 23%): IGT alone 17%, IFG alone 2%, both 1% — [Latt et al. 2019](https://www.dovepress.com/measurement-of-diabetes-prediabetes-and-their-associated-risk-factors--peer-reviewed-fulltext-article-DMSO)
- An earlier WDF national estimate was 5.7% in 2013, projected to 8% by 2035. The 2014 measured figure was nearly double it — [Latt et al. 2019](https://www.dovepress.com/measurement-of-diabetes-prediabetes-and-their-associated-risk-factors--peer-reviewed-fulltext-article-DMSO)
- The survey was funded as the "National Survey on prevalence of diabetes and NCDs" (WDF13-0772) — [World Diabetes Foundation](https://worlddiabetesfoundation.org/what-we-do/projects/wdf13-0772)

**Diabetes (IDF model estimates)**
- IDF Diabetes Atlas country page: adults 20–79 with diabetes were 2.1 million (2011), **2.4 million (2024)** and 3.3 million (2050 projection). Age-standardised prevalence is about 6.7% in 2024 (7.1% in 2011, 7.6% in 2050), but the column alignment was unclear in the extract. IDF's inputs are the 2009 MoH NCD survey and STEPS 2014, so even "2024" rests on 2009–2014 data — [IDF Diabetes Atlas, Myanmar](https://diabetesatlas.org/fr/data-by-location/country/myanmar/) (via search extract; the page would not load directly)
- IDF's "Key information" page cites 7.1% adult diabetes prevalence for 2021 — [IDF](https://idf.org/?p=5241)
- NOTE: IDF (~6.7–7.1%, 20–79) and STEPS (10.5%, 25–64) differ because of age range, method (capillary OGTT vs modelled) and age standardisation. Do not mix them.

**Overweight / obesity**
- STEPS 2014 (25–64, n = 8,390 analysed), WHO cut-offs: **overweight 20.4%, obesity 6.5%**. Mean BMI: rural 21.9 ± 4.1, urban 24.5 ± 5.5 — [Bjertness et al., BMJ Open 2021, PMC7929804](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7929804)
- Asian cut-offs, women, urban vs rural (STEPS 2014): overweight 35.4% vs 28.4%; obese 27.9% vs 12.7% — [Bjertness et al. 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7929804)
- Myanmar DHS 2015–16, women 18–49 (n = 12,160), Asian cut-offs: underweight 14.1%, **overweight 28.1%, obese 13.1%**. Women 30–49 had about 50% overweight or obesity — [Hong et al., PLOS ONE 2018, PMC5856399](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC5856399/)
- Global Nutrition Report (NCD-RisC modelled, adults 18+; year not shown in text, likely 2016): obesity women 9.0%, men 5.3% (regional average 10.3% / 7.5%); diabetes women 9.1%, men 8.3%. Myanmar is "off course" on every diet-related NCD target (obesity, diabetes, raised BP, sodium) — [Global Nutrition Report, Myanmar](https://globalnutritionreport.org/resources/nutrition-profiles/asia/south-eastern-asia/myanmar/)
- STEPS 2014: 94% of adults have at least one NCD lifestyle risk factor and 20% have three or more; 86% eat less than the recommended fruit and vegetables — via review summarised in search; primary record at [GHDx STEPS 2014](https://ghdx.healthdata.org/record/myanmar-steps-noncommunicable-disease-risk-factors-survey-2014)

**Hypertension**
- 2009 nationwide survey (ages 15–64, n ≈ 6,400+): prevalence 30.1% men, 29.8% women. Mean BMI was 21.7 in men and 23.0 in women; high BMI was linked to hypertension (OR 2.6 men, 2.3 women) — [Bjertness et al., BMC Public Health 2016, PMC4950687](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC4950687/)
- Meta-analysis (7 studies, n = 20,901): pooled prevalence 22% (men 21.5%, women 22.7%); awareness and control low — [Naing & Aung, Medicine 2014, PMC4616280](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4616280); [LWW full text](https://journals.lww.com/md-journal/fulltext/2014/11010/Prevalence_and_Risk_Factors_of_Hypertension_in.1.aspx)
- Yangon Region (ages 25–74, STEPS method): age-standardised prevalence rose from 26.7% (2004) to **34.6% (2014)**; mean systolic BP rose from 122.8 to 128.1 mmHg — [Htet et al., BMC Public Health 2017, PMC5659019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5659019)
- 2009 vs 2014 STEPS risk-factor comparison paper (raised BP, overweight, fruit/veg). Abstract numbers could not be retrieved (paywall/403) — [Aung et al., J Public Health 2019](https://academic.oup.com/jpubhealth/article-abstract/41/4/750/5132767)

### Inferences
- **Rough absolute numbers (my estimate, not sourced):** Myanmar has about 54–55 million people, roughly half aged 25–64 (about 27–29 million; base not verified in this research). Applying the 2014 STEPS rates gives roughly **2.8–3.0 million adults 25–64 with diabetes**, **~5.5 million with pre-diabetes** and **~7.5 million overweight or obese** (WHO cut-offs). At ~25–30%, hypertension would be about **7–9 million**. These are back-of-envelope figures; the only published absolute number is IDF's 2.4 million (20–79, 2024).
- At Asian BMI cut-offs (the right ones for a Burmese app), the overweight/obese share is far higher than the WHO-cut-off 27%. For urban women it is over 60% (35.4% + 27.9%).
- The combined pool of overweight, pre-diabetic, diabetic or hypertensive adults is plausibly over 10 million people, many overlapping. That is the addressable "diet-related risk" population, concentrated in towns.

### Gaps
- The WHO STEPS 2014 national report (who.int/ncds/surveillance/steps/Myanmar_2014_STEPS_Report.pdf) could not be retrieved; the old WHO link is dead and the medbox mirror is empty. **The national 2014 raised-blood-pressure figure for 25–64 and the official overweight figures by sex were therefore not verified.** A circulating "26.4%" raised-BP figure was not confirmed by any source.
- No national NCD survey after 2014 was found. Nothing from 2018–2026 at national scale.
- The IDF 11th edition (2025) Myanmar figures could not be confirmed directly; the 2024 values above come from the current IDF country page.
- No NCD-RisC hypertension or obesity estimates with years were retrieved.

## 2. Undiagnosed, unaware and uncontrolled

### Takeaway
**About 7 in 10 people with diabetes in 2014 did not know they had it.** In most regions under 30% were aware. Even in Yangon only about 28% of people with hypertension knew it, and treatment and control stayed low. A tracking app (and later glucose/BP logging) speaks to a mostly undiagnosed population.

### Cited Findings
- STEPS 2014: about 70% of people with diabetes were newly diagnosed by the survey. Awareness was under 30% in every region except Yangon — [Latt et al. 2019](https://www.dovepress.com/measurement-of-diabetes-prediabetes-and-their-associated-risk-factors--peer-reviewed-fulltext-article-DMSO)
- Yangon Region, diabetes 2004 → 2014: awareness rose from 44% to 69% and treatment from 55% to 69%, but the share with controlled diabetes did not change. Age-standardised prevalence was 8.3% → 10.2% (not significant) — [Aung et al., IJERPH 2019, PMC6765960](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6765960/); [MDPI](https://www.mdpi.com/1660-4601/16/18/3461)
- Yangon Region, hypertension 2004 → 2014: awareness rose from 19.4% to 27.8%; treatment and control "remain low" — [Htet et al. 2017](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5659019)
- Meta-analysis: hypertension awareness and control in Myanmar are low — [Naing & Aung 2014](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4616280)
- A 2025 protocol (Pyin Oo Lwin) says hypertension awareness, treatment and control have improved but uncontrolled hypertension "has not yet declined enough to meet global targets" — [protocols.io 2025](https://www.protocols.io/view/determinants-of-awareness-treatment-and-control-of-dym87u9w.html)

### Inferences
- With ~70% undiagnosed and IDF at 2.4M, roughly 1.5–2 million Myanmar adults may have diabetes without knowing it. This is my estimate, applying the 2014 share to IDF's 2024 count.
- Yangon's much better awareness, despite the highest prevalence, shows the urban, better-served, smartphone-owning segment is the most diagnosis-aware. That fits Sar AI's likely early adopters.

### Gaps
- No national hypertension awareness/treatment/control figure for 2014 was retrieved.
- No national diabetes control rate (HbA1c or FPG target) was found.

## 3. Urban vs rural, sex and age

### Takeaway
Diet-related disease in Myanmar is **urban, female-leaning (for obesity and pre-diabetes) and rises steeply with age**. **Yangon has the highest diabetes rate (18.2%)**. Urban diabetes is ~14% vs ~9% rural, and urban mean BMI is 2.5 units higher even after adjusting for income and education.

### Cited Findings
- Diabetes, 2014: urban 14.4% vs rural 9%; Yangon highest (18.2%), Nay Pyi Taw lowest (4.2%) — [Latt et al. 2019](https://www.dovepress.com/measurement-of-diabetes-prediabetes-and-their-associated-risk-factors--peer-reviewed-fulltext-article-DMSO)
- Diabetes by age, 2014: men 3.6% (25–34) → 19.5% (55–64); women 4.5% (25–34) → 23.7% (55–64). Age 55–64 vs 25–34: diabetes RRR 2.7, pre-diabetes RRR 6.4 — [Latt et al. 2019](https://www.dovepress.com/measurement-of-diabetes-prediabetes-and-their-associated-risk-factors--peer-reviewed-fulltext-article-DMSO)
- Risk factors, 2014: central obesity (waist ≥ 90 cm men / ≥ 80 cm women) RRR 1.4 for diabetes and 3.1 for pre-diabetes; female sex RRR 1.5 for diabetes; urban residence RRR 1.5 for pre-diabetes — [Latt et al. 2019](https://www.dovepress.com/measurement-of-diabetes-prediabetes-and-their-associated-risk-factors--peer-reviewed-fulltext-article-DMSO)
- BMI, 2014: urban residence associated with BMI +2.49 kg/m² (95% CI 2.28–2.70) after adjustment. Only 9–17% of that gap is explained by SES, income, education or employment. Mean BMI rises with SES (21.4 / 22.9 / 24.1) and education (21.9 / 22.9 / 24.2) — [Bjertness et al. 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7929804)
- Yangon combined overweight + obesity: urban 39.8% (2004) → 40.9% (2014); rural 23.0% → 31.2%, so rural areas are catching up — [Bjertness et al. 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7929804)
- Women 18–49 (DHS 2015–16): overweight or obesity peaks at about 50% in women 30–49 — [Hong et al. 2018](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC5856399/)
- Hypertension (2009): similar in men (30.1%) and women (29.8%) — [Bjertness et al. 2016](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC4950687/)
- Yangon hypertension rose in both urban and rural areas between 2004 and 2014 — [Htet et al. 2017](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5659019)

### Inferences
- The core user for a weight/diabetes-prevention app is an urban woman aged 30–55 in Yangon or Mandalay. She has the highest overweight/obesity and pre-diabetes rates and Yangon's highest awareness.
- The urban BMI excess is largely not explained by income. That points to urban diet and activity (more oil, sweetened drinks, street food, less manual work) as drivers, which supports a food-logging intervention.

### Gaps
- No Mandalay-specific prevalence figures were found.
- No sex-specific national overweight figures for men were retrieved; DHS 2015–16 men's BMI was not found.

## 4. Diet drivers: rice volume, oil, sugar in tea, salt (ngapi / fish sauce)

### Takeaway
Myanmar eats more rice per person than almost any country: **~155 kg/yr (2016 household survey) to ~279 kg/yr (2025 food-supply estimate)**. Rice supplies **roughly half to three-quarters of dietary energy** (48% in 2009 per IRRI; ~74% in an older FAO table). Salt intake and salt-taste thresholds are high, and fermented fish products are 10–30% salt. Hard data on cooking oil and sweetened tea were not found.

### Cited Findings
**Rice**
- Per-capita rice consumption ~278.97 kg/person/yr in 2025, about 3× the global median (80.5 kg), the highest in the world. This is an aggregator figure, likely from food-supply/balance-sheet data, so treat as an upper-bound estimate — [WorldoStats](https://worldostats.com/country-stats/rice-consumption-per-capita-by-country/); [World Population Review](https://worldpopulationreview.com/country-rankings/rice-consumption-by-country)
- A 2016 survey by the Myanmar Rice Federation and Yezin Agricultural University found people eat about 155 kg/yr, and a government push for a healthier, more diverse diet ("rice-mad Myanmar eyes healthier diet") — [Oman Observer / AFP](https://www.omanobserver.om/ampArticle/32419)
- Rice share of energy: 68.4% (1,451 kcal/day) in 1995 falling to 48.3% (1,204 kcal/day) in 2009 — [Ricepedia / IRRI Rice Almanac](https://ricepedia.org/index.php/myanmar)
- Older FAO table: rice supply 577.9 g/capita/day, 73.6% of dietary energy supply (circa early 2000s) — [FAO International Rice Commission](https://www.fao.org/4/Y6618E/Y6618E00.htm); [FAO Rice production, consumption and nutrition](https://www.fao.org/4/Y4347E/y4347e02.htm)
- Total dietary energy supply was 2,528 kcal/capita/day in 2011 — [FAOSTAT country profile Myanmar](https://data.opendevelopmentmekong.net/dataset/62fba15b-4d45-4ea3-b193-662a56046ddc/resource/ee0e3823-e544-45fa-aa60-762cf33d90c7/download/faostat-country-profile-myanmar.pdf)
- Meanwhile, 33% of the population lacked enough calories for an active life in 2024 (FAOSTAT), a reminder of the dual burden — [Food Systems Dashboard](https://www.foodsystemsdashboard.org/countries/mmr/fsci/printable)

**Salt / ngapi / fish sauce**
- Ngapi (fish/shrimp paste) contains 10–25% salt; fish sauce and salted fish 20–30% — [Global New Light of Myanmar](https://www.gnlm.com.mm/ngapi-made-from-myanmar-fish-enriches-taste-of-myanmar-meal-with-nutritional-values/)
- Salt content varies by region: Rakhine ngapi has little or none, while Ayeyarwady/Tanintharyi freshwater-fish ngapi is heavily salted — [Wikipedia: Ngapi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ngapi) (secondary)
- Myanmar adults had higher salt-taste thresholds, salt preference, spot-urine sodium and salt-use behaviour scores than Korean adults (small clinic sample, c. 2014) — [PubMed 27702732](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27702732)
- High salt intake was cited among drivers of the Yangon hypertension rise — [Htet et al. 2017](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s12889-017-4870-y)
- Myanmar lists salt reduction as a policy target but has no implemented salt-reduction policy; sodium intake is "off course" — [Global Nutrition Report](https://globalnutritionreport.org/resources/nutrition-profiles/asia/south-eastern-asia/myanmar/)

**Oil / fat / recipes**
- Reformulating common Myanmar recipes cut fat, saturated fat and sodium while staying acceptable to consumers, so traditional dishes are high in all three — [ScienceDirect 2021](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2352364621000262)
- Fruit and vegetable intake: 86% of adults below recommended amount (STEPS 2014) — via [GHDx STEPS 2014 record](https://ghdx.healthdata.org/record/myanmar-steps-noncommunicable-disease-risk-factors-survey-2014) (figure from review summary)

### Inferences
- The gap between 155 and 279 kg/yr is mostly method (household intake vs food supply incl. waste and feed). At 155 kg/yr (about 425 g raw/day, roughly 1,500 kcal), rice still provides about half or more of a 2,500 kcal diet. Rice portion size is therefore the single biggest calorie lever, which suits Sar AI's "plates" unit.
- Since a third of the population is calorie-insecure, the app's calorie-reduction framing fits the urban middle class; rural or poor users face undernutrition, not excess.

### Gaps
- **No figures found for cooking-oil use per capita**, despite the famously oily "si pyan" curries. FAOSTAT vegetable-oil supply should be checked.
- **No studies found on 3-in-1 tea/coffee mix or sugar in laphet yay (tea shop tea)**, nor on sugar intake per capita.
- No national 24-h urine sodium estimate for Myanmar was found.
- No source for "typical plates of rice per day".
- No data on street-food share of meals.

## 5. Trends since 2014 and effects of the 2021 coup / COVID

### Takeaway
Up to 2014 the trend was clearly upward: hypertension and rural overweight were rising in Yangon, and measured diabetes was double earlier estimates. **After the February 2021 coup, NCD care was badly disrupted**: medicines cost 3–4× more, clinics are harder to reach, and supply is blocked in conflict areas. No post-coup prevalence data exist.

### Cited Findings
- Yangon hypertension 26.7% (2004) → 34.6% (2014); diabetes 8.3% → 10.2% (not significant); rural overweight/obesity 23.0% → 31.2% — [Htet et al. 2017](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5659019); [Aung et al. 2019](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6765960/); [Bjertness et al. 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7929804)
- IDF diabetes count 2.1M (2011) → 2.4M (2024) → 3.3M (2050 projected) — [IDF Diabetes Atlas](https://diabetesatlas.org/fr/data-by-location/country/myanmar/)
- After the coup, people must travel to other towns for routine hypertension and diabetes drugs. Medicine and supply prices rose 3–4×, and aid blockages raise chronic-disease risk — [J Glob Health commentary 2023, PMC9830275](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9830275/)
- WHO Myanmar supports community workers, mobile clinics and health posts screening tens of thousands for hypertension and diabetes in conflict-affected areas — [WHO Myanmar](https://www.who.int/myanmar/news/feature-stories/detail/ensuring-the-most-vulnerable-population-receive-the-care-they-need)
- A community-led project screened over 14,000 people for hypertension/diabetes in June–Dec 2022 — [Geneva Health Forum 2024 abstract](https://ghf.unige.ch/event/1/contributions/205/)
- A cluster RCT of a village-health-worker CVD-risk model is running in conflict-affected eastern Myanmar — [ClinicalTrials.gov NCT06819839](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT06819839)
- In Yangon, NCDs are often neglected because of low awareness and fear of long-term treatment costs (NGO anecdote) — [CPI](https://cpintl.org/impact-area/health/making-ncd-care-affordable-it-got-so-bad-i-had-to-have-my-leg-amputated)
- A 2025 Yangon study (n = 512, aged 40+) reports diabetes among older residents rising from 14.6% to 31.9% between 2004 and 2014 (as cited in the paper) — [J Diabetes Metab Disord 2025](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s40200-025-01768-5)

### Inferences
- With clinic care costlier and harder to reach since 2021, low-cost self-management tools (diet tracking that works offline on cheap phones) gain relative value. This is an inference, not evidence of demand.
- Food-price inflation after 2021 likely pushed poorer households toward cheaper calories (rice, oil), but no source was found confirming this shift.

### Gaps
- No post-2021 national survey of NCD prevalence, medicine availability or diet change.
- A search summary mentioned a qualitative study citing hypertension 50% and diabetes 18% "until 2023" among older people. Its primary source could not be identified, so it is not used.
- No data on COVID-era weight gain or diet change in Myanmar.

## 6. Thailand comparison and Burmese diaspora

### Takeaway
Thailand's measured diabetes rate (10.1% in 2020, 38% unaware) is similar to Myanmar's 2014 figure. Thai hypertension (25.7% in 2019–20) is in the same range, with control only 22.7%. Burmese migrant workers in Thailand show **27.8% hypertension**, with risk 6× higher if overweight. In the US, Burmese refugees from urban backgrounds have much higher obesity, diabetes and hypertension than camp-origin refugees.

### Cited Findings
**Thailand**
- Diabetes (NHES, age- and sex-standardised): 7.5% (2004) → **10.1% (2020)**; only 62.8% aware — [Diabetes trends among Thai adults 2004–2020, Mahidol repository](https://repository.li.mahidol.ac.th/items/0746c95d-bb6d-47e6-9201-fbea60bb3617/full)
- Undiagnosed diabetes in Thailand was 51.2% (men) and 41.3% (women) in 2014 — via [Mahidol repository search results](https://repository.li.mahidol.ac.th/handle/123456789/12368)
- Hypertension (NHES VI 2019–20): age-standardised 25.7% (men 26.8%, women 24.6%), roughly flat since 2004. Awareness 51.5%, treated 47.9%, controlled 22.7% (down from ~30% in 2014) — [Trends in hypertension in Thailand 2004–2020, PMC11562084](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11562084/)
- Older Thai adults (NHES 4–6): obesity, diabetes and hypertension rose 2008–2020. In 2020 about half had hypertension, ~20% diabetes and ~33% obesity — [IJHS / NHES older adults](https://li05.tci-thaijo.org/index.php/IJHS/article/view/813)

**Diaspora**
- Myanmar migrant workers, Chiang Mai, Dec 2017 (n = 414): **hypertension 27.8%**, overweight 16.4%, obese 4.3% (WHO cut-offs). Overweight aOR 5.88 and obese aOR 6.10 for hypertension; also age, male sex (aOR 2.42) and alcohol (aOR 2.80) — [Aung et al., IJERPH 2022, PMC8955730](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8955730/)
- Health literacy and high blood pressure among Myanmar migrant workers in NE Thailand (2024) — [PLOS ONE 2024](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0302057) (prevalence not extracted)
- Burmese type 2 diabetes outpatients in Samut Sakhon: 78.1% had metabolic syndrome — via search summary of [Thai Journal (jhsmr)](https://jhsmr.org/index.php/jhsmr/article/download/882/938)
- Hyperglycaemia study of 402 Myanmar migrants in Chiang Rai (2022); prevalence not extracted — [TCI ThaiJo](https://li01.tci-thaijo.org/index.php/JBAP/article/view/260736)
- US refugees from Burma: urban-origin vs camp-origin obesity 18.0% vs 5.9%, diabetes 6.5% vs 0.8%, hypertension 12.7% vs 8.1% — [J Immigrant & Minority Health, via Najah portal](https://research-portal.najah.edu/migrant/9253/)

### Inferences
- Myanmar (2014) and Thailand (2020) have similar diabetes and hypertension rates, but Thailand's universal-coverage system finds more cases. Myanmar likely has a larger undiagnosed share (70% vs ~37%).
- Migrants in Thailand (several million Burmese) are a reachable Burmese-language segment with measurable hypertension risk tied to weight.

### Gaps
- No current Thai national adult obesity (NHES VI) figure and no IDF 2024/2025 Thailand count were retrieved.
- No health data found for Burmese diaspora in Singapore or Malaysia.

## 7. Government and NGO NCD/diet programmes in Myanmar

### Takeaway
Myanmar's main NCD programme is **WHO PEN** (Package of Essential NCD interventions): it was piloted in 2 Yangon townships in 2012, reached 20 townships in 2017 and was **nominally nationwide (330 townships) by end-2019**, then disrupted by COVID and the coup. There was an NCD strategic plan for 2017–2021. **No evidence was found of national food-based dietary guidelines or "rice plate" guidance.**

### Cited Findings
- PEN pilot started 2012 in Hlegu and Hmawbi (Yangon). It expanded to 20 townships in 5 states/regions in March 2017 and was implemented nationwide (330 townships) at the end of 2019. Clinics receive essential NCD medicines and BP/glucose equipment — [EU capacity4dev PEN evaluation](https://capacity4dev.europa.eu/discussions/sphc-myanmar-implementation-package-essential-non-communicable-pen-disease-interventions-preliminary-evaluation_en); [PMC7028297](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7028297)
- PEN problems: high loss to follow-up, poor CVD-risk recording, shortages of medicines and equipment, weak health-worker skills. Fieldwork in 2019 found hypertension medicine short while others were overstocked — [EU capacity4dev](https://capacity4dev.europa.eu/media/103408/download/571c6f72-acb0-400c-80bc-d89229e54244_en); [PMC9659547](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9659547)
- National Strategic Plan for Prevention and Control of NCDs 2017–2021; NCDs a priority in the National Health Plan 2017–2021 — [PMC7028297](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7028297)
- WHO PEN training-of-trainers manual for township medical officers — [WHO extranet](https://extranet.who.int/ncdccs/Data/MMR_D1_TOT%20PEN%20manual%20for%20TMO%20.pdf)
- WDF project "Expanding access to primary health care for NCDs" — [WDF15-1286](https://www.worlddiabetesfoundation.org/what-we-do/projects/wdf15-1286/)
- Myanmar Diabetes Association exists as the professional/patient body — [Myanmar Diabetes Association](https://myanmardiabetes.org.mm/about-us/)
- National Plan of Action for Food and Nutrition 2011–2015 (draft) — [FAOLEX](https://faolex.fao.org/docs/pdf/mya145314.pdf)
- A government-backed push toward diet diversity away from rice (c. 2016) — [Oman Observer / AFP](https://www.omanobserver.om/ampArticle/32419)
- State media health messaging on diabetes prevention — [MOI "Let's prevent Diabetes Mellitus"](https://www.moi.gov.mm/moi:eng/article/12144)

### Inferences
- PEN gives Myanmar a primary-care screening framework (BP, glucose) but no structured diet-coaching tool. A Burmese food-logging app could complement PEN referrals rather than compete with them.
- Without official plate/portion guidance, Sar AI's "plates" unit and calorie targets are a de facto standard. Nutrition values must stay clearly labelled as estimates (consistent with the project rule that mock numbers are illustrative).

### Gaps
