# Competitive landscape: calorie tracking and diet management for Myanmar (Sar AI)

Research date: 2026-11-01. Budget-limited pass (about 15 search/fetch calls). Many pricing sources are SEO/affiliate blogs run by competing apps (Nutrola, NutriScan, Welling, etc.). They are flagged where used. Official pricing pages and Play listings could not be read directly: the Google Play page for Binfo returned no content, and Appfigures' Myanmar chart returned HTTP 403.

## Global AI/calorie trackers: features, pricing, Burmese support, Myanmar food fit, scale

### Takeaway
The global market is consolidating around AI photo logging. MyFitnessPal bought Cal AI in early 2026 (Cal AI: more than 15M downloads and more than $30M annual revenue, per MyFitnessPal). US prices run from about $24 to $150 a year. No source found shows Burmese UI support or meaningful Myanmar-dish coverage in any of these apps. Myanmar-dish entries exist only as scattered SEO nutrition pages (e.g., SnapCalorie's "Burmese chicken curry").

### Cited Findings
**Cal AI / MyFitnessPal (deal and scale)**
- On 2026-03-02, TechCrunch reported that MyFitnessPal had acquired Cal AI, the photo-based calorie app built by two teenage founders, after about a year of negotiations. — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/03/02/myfitnesspal-has-acquired-cal-ai-the-viral-calorie-app-built-by-teens)
- Per MyFitnessPal, Cal AI reached more than 15M downloads and more than $30M annual revenue in under two years. The 7-person team, including CEO Zach Yadegari, was retained. Cal AI will run independently and keep its photo-estimation focus. It has been connected to MyFitnessPal's 20M-food database. MFP CEO Mike Fisher positions Cal AI as "speed and simplicity" and MFP as detailed and accurate tracking. — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/03/02/myfitnesspal-has-acquired-cal-ai-the-viral-calorie-app-built-by-teens); [Athletech News](https://athletechnews.com/myfitnesspal-cal-ai-acquisition)
- Revenue estimates conflict. Latka reports about $40M annual revenue (March 2026, citing Yahoo Finance). Techleap's headline says "$40M in annual sales". A case-study site claims "$0 to $50M+ ARR in 18 months" (unverified marketing). — [Latka](https://getlatka.com/companies/calai.app/team); [Techleap](https://finder.techleap.nl/news/note/myfitnesspal-acquires-teen-built-cal-ai-after-40m-in-annual-sales); [Superframeworks](https://superframeworks.com/case-study/cal-ai). Treat MFP's own figure (>$30M) as the floor.
- Close date conflicts. A Spanish-language report citing Business Insider says the deal closed in December 2025 and was announced in March 2026. — [Ecosistema Startup](https://ecosistemastartup.com/?p=100894)
- Appfigures estimated about $2M of consumer spending in Cal AI in a single month (February 2025), its biggest month at that point. — [Appfigures](https://appfigures.com/resources/insights/20250228/4-this-is-where-ai-shines-(and-generates-millions))
- A law firm's press release says MyFitnessPal is a Francisco Partners portfolio company. — [Benesch](https://www.beneschlaw.com/news/benesch-advises-myfitnesspal-on-acquisition-of-cal-ai-the-viral-calorie-app/)
- Sensor Tower expects global in-app purchase revenue from AI apps to exceed $4B in H1 2026 and names Cal AI among the leading health and fitness AI players. — [Sensor Tower blog](https://sensortower.com/blog/health-and-fitness-apps-ai)

**Language and Asian-food fit**
- FatSecret added Thai and Vietnamese UI languages (App Store release notes). Burmese is not mentioned. — [FatSecret App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/calorie-counter-by-fatsecret/id347184248)
- FatSecret's database returns more than 1,000 results for "Rad Thai", which shows deep Thai coverage by crowdsourcing. — [FatSecret search](https://android.fatsecret.com/calories-nutrition/search?q=Rad+Thai)
- Complaint evidence (vendor-sourced): MFP's crowdsourced database has 5 different "pad thai" entries ranging from 300 to 800 kcal. An app that returns "food not found" or defaults to a generic entry for a dish not in its database is "less useful" for Southeast Asian food. — [Nutrola (competitor blog)](https://nutrola.app/en/blog/best-calorie-tracking-app-for-thai-vietnamese-filipino-food-2026); [Welling (competitor blog)](https://www.welling.ai/articles/best-calorie-tracking-apps-southeast-asia-2026)
- Myanmar dishes appear only as scattered web nutrition pages, e.g., SnapCalorie's "Burmese chicken curry" page. — [SnapCalorie](https://www.snapcalorie.com/nutrition/burmese_chicken_curry_nutrition.html)
- Foodakl (a photo tracker) publishes calories and macros for 65 Burmese dishes, with a median of about 300 kcal. — [Foodakl](https://foodakl.com/kitchens/burmese/)
- Other new "Asian food" AI trackers exist, e.g., Bao ("AI Calorie Tracker for Asian Food"). — [Bao](https://trybao.app/)

### Inferences
- Photo AI is now table stakes and cheap in the US (about $30 a year for Cal AI). It is not a moat in itself. Sar AI's advantage has to come from Burmese language, Myanmar dish recognition and portion units (plates and spoons of rice, shared curries), and local payment.
- US prices of $30 to $150 a year are steep relative to Myanmar incomes. Store payment friction (international cards) likely makes them impractical for most Myanmar users regardless of features.
- The acquisition shows that an AI-first, simple tracker can be built with a tiny team and sold to an incumbent.

### Gaps
- Could not verify official per-country prices for Myanmar or Thailand for any app. Play Store regional pricing was not accessible.
- Could not verify whether each global app can be installed from Google Play in Myanmar. Play listings could not be fetched.
- No data found on photo/AI features, pricing or language specifics for Cronometer, MacroFactor, Samsung Health or Google Fit in this pass. From memory and unverified: Cronometer and MacroFactor are database/macro-centric and US/EU oriented, Samsung Health is free and preinstalled on Samsung phones, and Google has been winding down Google Fit APIs in favour of Health Connect. All of this needs checking.
- No source confirms Burmese UI in any of the 12 global apps. The absence was not positively verified, e.g., from each app's language list.
- No download data for any of these apps in Myanmar specifically (Sensor Tower/Appfigures country data are paywalled or blocked).

## Local Myanmar options (apps, telehealth, dietitians, creators, gyms)

### Takeaway
At least one direct local competitor exists: Binfo, by Earthlike Co., Ltd., Yangon. It bills itself as the first calorie tracker made for Myanmar and claims "10,000+ verified Myanmar food data". No photo/AI logging or pricing was found for it. Telehealth apps (MyanCare, OnDoctor, HEAL by Pun Hlaing) cover general medicine, not diet tracking. The weight-loss content scene is Burmese YouTube/Facebook diet vlogs. No data was found on dietitian pricing.

### Cited Findings
**Burmese calorie apps**
- Binfo (Earthlike Co., Ltd., No. 36 Thantaman St, Dagon Tsp, Yangon): calorie calculator, macro calculator, serving guide, progress tracking, activity tracking, and "10,000+ Verified Myanmar Food Data". Available on Google Play, the App Store and APKPure. It has a "BINFO TV" YouTube channel plus Facebook, Instagram and LinkedIn, and its site invites sign-ups for "soft launch events". The site shows no pricing, payment methods, photo/AI features or user counts. — [binfo.app](https://binfo.app/)
- Binfo's Play listing describes it as the first calorie tracker developed for the people of Myanmar. This comes from the search snippet; the listing page itself could not be fetched. — [Google Play: Binfo](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.earthlike.binfo&hl=en)
- A hobby open-source Android calorie app on GitHub ("zixora-calories") has a Burmese UI, works offline and has a Thadingyut theme. Its maturity is unknown. — [GitHub](https://github.com/SHO101234/zixora-calories)

**Telehealth**
- MyanCare: describes itself as a leading telehealth app in Myanmar, with video, audio and text consultations with 700+ doctors and specialists and medicine delivery within 24 hours. Fees not found. — [MyanCare](https://myancare.org/); [MyanCare Facebook](https://www.facebook.com/Myancareapps/)
- OnDoctor (Yangon): paid daily, weekly or monthly packages for unlimited chat questions, plus scheduled video calls with "premium doctors". Prices not shown in the listing. — [OnDoctor](https://ondoctor.com.mm/); [Google Play: OnDoctor](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.mm.ondoctor&hl=en)
- HEAL by Pun Hlaing Hospitals: hospital telehealth app with home delivery of prescribed medicines within Yangon. — [Pun Hlaing](https://punhlainghospitals.com/heal-app/)
- Dr. Galen directory: online consultations with Myanmar doctors, mostly listed at USD 25 for 10 to 30 minutes. — [Dr. Galen](https://drgalen.org/myanmar-doctors)
- International SOS (Yangon clinic) and Bumrungrad Clinic Yangon also offer teleconsultation (expat or premium segment). — [International SOS](https://www.internationalsos.com/locations/myanmar/yangon); [Bumrungrad Yangon](https://www.bumrungrad.com/en/yangon)

**Creators and social**
- Burmese-language weight-loss vlogs exist on YouTube, e.g., "lost 15 lb — how I cooked and ate" and the #naikovlog weekly diet vlog with #myweightlossjourney hashtags. — [YouTube 1](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ez02wc8Zlbs); [YouTube 2](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=nFDA_WDtwS0)
- Influencer directories list Myanmar food and fitness creators (Modash, Heepsy), but the fitness list is noisy (mostly unrelated channels). — [Modash](https://www.modash.io/find-influencers/myanmar/food); [Heepsy](https://www.heepsy.com/top-youtube/fitness/myanmar)

**Gyms (dated)**
- 2019 snapshot: Balance Fitness was billed as Yangon's first premium gym. YGN Gym had 10 Yangon branches, with membership from 132,000 MMK for the first 3 months. Real Fitness started at USD 120. These prices are about 7 years old and likely stale given inflation and kyat depreciation. — [Myanmore 2019](https://www.myanmore.com/2019/02/top-7-gyms-in-yangon/)
- F1 Fitness at Novotel Yangon Max advertises "affordable membership programs" (no price shown). — [Novotel Yangon Max](https://www.novotelyangonmax.com/recreation/f1-fitness/)

### Inferences
- Binfo is the closest direct competitor: Burmese-market, food-database-led, apparently without AI photo logging. Its "soft launch" language suggests it is early-stage. Sar AI should benchmark against it directly: install it, count dishes, and check pricing and payment.
- Telehealth apps could be partners (dietitian referral, diabetes patients) rather than competitors. None appears to offer self-serve food logging.
- Burmese weight-loss demand is visible in creator content. Influencer marketing on YouTube, Facebook and TikTok is a plausible acquisition channel.

### Gaps
- No Burmese-language diabetes management app found. Not searched in depth.
- No Myanmar dietitian or nutritionist service prices found. Hospital dietitian consults (e.g., Pun Hlaing, Asia Royal, SSC) need direct inquiry.
- No current (2025–2026) Yangon gym prices or chain list. No Myanmar-specific evidence found on keto or intermittent fasting trends. The search engine handled Burmese-script queries poorly.
- No Burmese Facebook weight-loss pages or groups identified by name, and no follower counts. No TikTok data.
- Binfo's downloads, rating, pricing, payment methods and whether it uses AI: all unverified.
- Myanmar Google Play top health and fitness chart: Appfigures blocked access (403).

## Thai options and how Thai users track food

### Takeaway
Thailand has several Thai-language commercial trackers on Google Play (CalCounter, CalTrack, "Calorie Diary" with AI food scan, Kalguroo) and university prototypes for Thai-dish image recognition. FatSecret has added Thai UI. No official Thai government or Department of Health food-tracking app and no LINE-based calorie service was found in this pass.

### Cited Findings
- CalCounter (Google Play): calorie counter designed specifically for Thai users, with Thai and English; Bangkok address. — [Google Play: CalCounter](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.cpmatch.calcounter&hl=en)
- CalTrack (Google Play): lists "authentic Thai dishes" with calories, protein, carbs and fat; located in Thailand. — [Google Play: CalTrack](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.caltrack.app)
- แคลอรี่ ไดอารี่ (Calorie Diary): Thai-language app with "AI food scan" in its title. — [Google Play: Calorie Diary](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.dimo.util.caloriediary)
- Kalguroo: AI calorie tracker on Google Play. Its Thai relevance is unverified. — [Google Play: Kalguroo](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.kalguroo.kalguroo_app.kalguroo_app&hl=en)
- Fastcal: AI photo calorie app advertising "Thai Food AI Optimization". — [MWM](https://mwm.ai/apps/fastcal-ai-calorie-tracker/1334702443)
- Academic projects: Kalrify (KMUTT senior project, Thai dish image → calories), KaKaloly (ML web app that classifies Thai food images, SSRU), and a 2024 Thai journal paper on an iOS calorie tracker built from requirements gathered from 78 Thai adults aged 18–65. — [KMUTT Kalrify](https://seniorproject.sit.kmutt.ac.th/showproject/CS62-BU58); [SSRU](https://library.ssru.ac.th/en/news/view/jn21046601); [JIST paper](https://ph02.tci-thaijo.org/index.php/JIST/article/view/252435)
- FatSecret added Thai-language UI. — [FatSecret App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/calorie-counter-by-fatsecret/id347184248)
- Thai Chana, the Department of Disease Control's COVID app, is unrelated to food and has been unavailable since 2025-10-01. It is noted only to show no government health-app precedent was found for food. — [Wikipedia: Thai Chana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thai_Chana)

### Inferences
- Thailand is more crowded than Myanmar: Thai UI in global apps, local Thai apps and Thai-dish AI research. For a later Thai launch, the wedge is likely Burmese migrant workers in Thailand (a diaspora play) rather than Thai nationals.

### Gaps
- No download or user figures for any Thai-specific app.
- Not found: Thai Department of Health or Thai hospital nutrition apps, LINE Official Account or LINE mini-app calorie services, Thai pricing. A Thai-language search is needed.
- No survey data on how Thai users actually track food.

## Food composition data for Myanmar dishes

### Takeaway
No public, downloadable Myanmar national food composition table was found online. Myanmar is an ASEANFOODS/INFOODS member, with a country coordinator at the Department of Medical Research, Yangon. The ASEAN tables are hosted by Mahidol INMU, but the online version was last updated in 2014 and draws on six national tables (Myanmar is not clearly among them). The FAO Myanmar Food Atlas (2022) gives photo-based portion sizes. A Mahidol 2023 paper references a "Myanmar food composition database".

### Cited Findings
- ASEANFOODS was established in 1986. Mahidol's Institute of Nutrition (INMU) has been the Regional Centre and Regional INFOODS database centre since 1991. Cambodia, Laos and Myanmar joined in 2001. — [INMU ASEANFOODS intro](https://inmu.mahidol.ac.th/aseanfoods/intro.html)
- FAO/INFOODS lists Myanmar as a member, with a country coordinator at the Department of Medical Research, Yangon. — [FAO INFOODS ASEANFOODS](https://www.fao.org/infoods/infoods/regional-data-centres/aseanfoods/en/)
- The Electronic ASEAN Food Composition Database is "systematically compiled from six ASEAN national food composition tables". The page was last updated 2014-02-23. — [INMU composition data](https://inmu.mahidol.ac.th/aseanfoods/composition_data.html)
- The INMU publications list names national tables for Malaysia, the Philippines, Singapore, Thailand, Vietnam and Indonesia, plus "ASEAN Food Composition Tables 2000". None is listed for Myanmar. — [INMU publications](https://inmu.mahidol.ac.th/aseanfoods/publication.html)
- FAO Myanmar Food Atlas (2022): a photo-based portion-size guide for commonly eaten Myanmar foods. It groups foods into 6 food groups with similar nutrient values and references various composition tables. — [FAO](https://openknowledge.fao.org/server/api/core/bitstreams/7b1ed1b8-4339-4bdd-ab55-8e6e3d5e2a08/content)
- Mahidol (2023): a "Myanmar Protein and Calorie Counting Booklet" for CKD patients. It used a Myanmar food composition database across 12 food groups, recalculated against Myanmar, ASEAN and some Indian composition databases, covering energy, protein, potassium and sodium. — [Mahidol repository](https://repository.li.mahidol.ac.th/items/038f4f02-1edc-4a82-9e19-85a9eabcd998/full)
- An academic study measured consumer acceptance of reduced calorie, fat, saturated fat and sodium versions of Myanmar recipes. It is a potential source of recipe-level nutrient data. — [DOAJ](https://doaj.org/article/e757b88f757d4218b849b1d40bd5f8ae)
- WorldFish (2020) analysed regional Myanmar consumption patterns and diet gaps using IHLCA 2010 household survey data. This is consumption data, not composition data. — [WorldFish](https://digitalarchive.worldfishcenter.org/items/fa60cdd5-85aa-4f90-aa27-61ffb24bfb7d/full)
- FAO food balance sheets give per-capita supply (e.g., one site citing FAO gives 2,809 kcal per person per day in 2023 food supply). This is supply data, not intake data. — [TriageMethod citing FAO](https://triagemethod.com/tools/human-nutrition-explorer/atlas/myanmar.html)

### Inferences
- The most practical data stack for Sar AI: (1) the FAO Myanmar Food Atlas for portion photos and sizes, which maps well to rice-plate units; (2) ASEAN, Thai (INMU) and USDA tables for ingredients; (3) recipe-based calculation for composite dishes (mohinga, laphet thoke, curries); (4) outreach to the Department of Medical Research (Yangon) for the national table. Binfo's "10,000+ verified" claim suggests a competitor has already compiled something similar.

### Gaps
- Not determined: whether a Myanmar national FCT exists in print or PDF (likely from the Department of Medical Research or the National Nutrition Centre), its licence, or whether ASEAN 2000 includes Myanmar entries.
- Atlas licence terms for commercial reuse not checked.

## Gaps a Myanmar-specific app could fill; evidence of complaints

### Takeaway
The evidence supports a gap for a Burmese-language, Myanmar-dish-aware, low-cost tracker. Global apps lack Burmese UI and depend on crowdsourced or generic entries that handle Asian composite dishes poorly. The one local competitor found (Binfo) appears database-led without AI photo logging. Direct user complaints specific to Myanmar food were not found; the available complaint evidence concerns Southeast Asian food generally and comes from competitor blogs.

### Cited Findings
- Crowdsourced inconsistency: 5 "pad thai" entries in MFP range from 300 to 800 kcal. — [Nutrola (competitor blog)](https://nutrola.app/en/blog/best-calorie-tracking-app-for-thai-vietnamese-filipino-food-2026)
- Apps that return "food not found" or a generic default for unlisted Southeast Asian dishes are less useful (vendor framing). — [Welling (competitor blog)](https://www.welling.ai/articles/best-calorie-tracking-apps-southeast-asia-2026)
- The Myanmar Food Atlas exists precisely because portion estimation for Myanmar foods needs local reference photos. — [FAO Myanmar Food Atlas](https://openknowledge.fao.org/server/api/core/bitstreams/7b1ed1b8-4339-4bdd-ab55-8e6e3d5e2a08/content)
- A local hobbyist built an offline Burmese calorie app, which signals felt demand for offline and Burmese-language use. — [GitHub](https://github.com/SHO101234/zixora-calories)
- Global app prices are $24 to $150 a year in USD (see section 1). — [TheBiteTracker](https://thebitetracker.com/field-notes/cheapest-calorie-tracking-apps)

### Inferences
- Candidate differentiators: Burmese UI and voice input; rice measured in plates or spoons; shared-curry and family-style portioning; tea-shop items; offline-first (power and internet cuts); pricing in kyat via local wallets (KBZPay/WavePay) instead of the store-only USD subscriptions global apps sell; AI photo logging tuned to Myanmar dishes, which Binfo does not appear to offer.
- Diaspora angle: Burmese workers in Thailand, Malaysia and Singapore, and communities in the US, are poorly served by Thai-only or English-only apps.

### Gaps
- No Play Store review mining was done to find Myanmar users complaining about global apps. Reading reviews of MFP and Cal AI from Myanmar is recommended.
- No data on Myanmar smartphone, health-app usage or willingness to pay was gathered in this pass.
- Myanmar access to Google Play paid subscriptions, and whether local wallets can be used for in-app billing: not verified.
