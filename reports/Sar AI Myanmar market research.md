# Sar AI wins by counting Myanmar's rice plates

Sar AI's best first customer is an **urban woman aged roughly 30–55 in Yangon or Mandalay**. She is worried about her weight, and often about prediabetes or diabetes in herself or a parent. She already looks for health advice and buys health products on Facebook, and she pays with KBZPay. The problem is large and real. Measured in 2014, **10.5% of adults aged 25–64 had diabetes, about 20% had prediabetes, and 27% were overweight or obese even by lenient WHO cut-offs**. **About 7 in 10 diabetics did not know they had it**. Rice, the biggest calorie source, supplies around half of all dietary energy. No global calorie app offers Burmese or handles a shared Myanmar meal well. The one local rival found, Binfo, appears to be a database-led tracker without photo AI. The market is hard. Inflation hit **24.6% in April 2026**, **poverty is 29.9%**, and the kyat trades at **2,100 officially but around 3,700–4,650 in practice**. Freedom House scores Myanmar's internet **9/100**, with 105 shutdowns in 2025, and Google Play cannot take local wallet payments. At **4,900 Ks a month (about $1.05–2.33)**, the Premium pass is affordable for salaried urban households but ambitious for minimum-wage earners. It should be priced as a prepaid, short, often-repriced product, and revenue should be planned on **1–2% paid conversion**. The biggest unknowns are whether people will log food daily at all, whether selling wallet passes inside an Android app breaks Google Play policy, and how Myanmar people actually split a shared meal. These are the things to validate first.

## Diet-linked disease touches over 10 million adults, and rice is the lever

Everything below rests on one awkward fact. **The latest nationally measured NCD data for Myanmar come from the WHO STEPS survey of September–December 2014** (adults 25–64, n = 8,757). Since then there are only models and small local studies, and no national survey has been run since the 2021 coup. Treat the 2014 figures as a floor. Urban weight and blood pressure were rising when they were taken.

In that survey, national diabetes prevalence was **10.5%** and prediabetes **19.7%**. Prediabetes was 23% in women and 16.5% in men ([Latt et al., DMSO 2019](https://www.dovepress.com/measurement-of-diabetes-prediabetes-and-their-associated-risk-factors--peer-reviewed-fulltext-article-DMSO)). The paper's own abstract gives slightly different sex splits, so which sex has more diabetes is unsettled. The higher prediabetes rate in women is consistent. The disease is concentrated in towns: **urban 14.4% against rural 9%, and Yangon highest at 18.2%**. It also climbs steeply with age, reaching 23.7% among women aged 55–64 (same source). The IDF models **2.4 million adults with diabetes in 2024, rising to 3.3 million by 2050** ([IDF Diabetes Atlas](https://diabetesatlas.org/fr/data-by-location/country/myanmar/)). Its inputs, though, are the same 2009–2014 surveys.

Overweight is worse than the headline suggests. With WHO cut-offs, STEPS 2014 found **20.4% overweight and 6.5% obese**. Using the Asian cut-offs that suit a Burmese app, **35.4% of urban women were overweight and another 27.9% obese**, more than 6 in 10. Urban mean BMI was 2.5 units higher than rural even after adjusting for income and education ([Bjertness et al., BMJ Open 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7929804)). That urban gap points to diet and activity, which is exactly what food logging addresses. The 2015–16 DHS found about **half of women aged 30–49 overweight or obese** ([Hong et al., PLOS ONE 2018](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC5856399/)). Hypertension in Yangon rose from **26.7% in 2004 to 34.6% in 2014** ([Htet et al., BMC Public Health 2017](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5659019)). Nationally it was about 30% in 2009 ([Bjertness et al., BMC Public Health 2016](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC4950687/)).

Much of this goes unnoticed. In 2014 **about 70% of people with diabetes were diagnosed by the survey itself**, and awareness was below 30% in every region except Yangon ([Latt et al. 2019](https://www.dovepress.com/measurement-of-diabetes-prediabetes-and-their-associated-risk-factors--peer-reviewed-fulltext-article-DMSO)). Even in Yangon, only 27.8% of people with hypertension knew it ([Htet et al. 2017](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5659019)). The coup made care harder. People now travel to other towns for routine diabetes and blood-pressure drugs, and **medicine prices rose 3–4×** ([J Glob Health 2023](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9830275/)). That raises the value of a cheap self-management tool, though it is not proof of demand.

Rice is the single biggest dietary lever. Food-supply estimates put Myanmar's consumption at about **279 kg per person a year, the highest in the world and about 3× the global median** ([Worldostats](https://worldostats.com/country-stats/rice-consumption-per-capita-by-country/); [World Population Review](https://worldpopulationreview.com/country-rankings/rice-consumption-by-country)). A 2016 household survey measured a lower **155 kg** that people actually eat ([Oman Observer/AFP](https://www.omanobserver.om/ampArticle/32419)). The gap is mostly method, since supply figures include waste. Either way rice supplied **48% of dietary energy in 2009**, down from 68% in 1995 ([Ricepedia/IRRI](https://ricepedia.org/index.php/myanmar)). Salt is the second lever. Ngapi contains 10–25% salt and fish sauce 20–30% ([GNLM](https://www.gnlm.com.mm/ngapi-made-from-myanmar-fish-enriches-taste-of-myanmar-meal-with-nutritional-values/)). Common Myanmar recipes are high in fat, saturated fat and sodium ([ScienceDirect 2021](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2352364621000262)). There are no hard data on cooking oil per person or on sugar in tea-shop tea. Myanmar also has no official food-based dietary or "rice plate" guidance, so Sar AI's plates unit would effectively become the standard.

A rough sizing, our estimate rather than a sourced figure: apply the 2014 rates to the roughly 27–29 million adults aged 25–64 in a population of 54.9 million ([DataReportal 2026](https://datareportal.com/reports/digital-2026-myanmar)). That gives about **3 million with diabetes, 5.5 million with prediabetes, 7.5 million overweight or obese (WHO cut-offs) and 7–9 million with hypertension**. The overlapping pool with a medical reason to watch rice, sugar and salt is **plausibly over 10 million adults**, concentrated in towns. One counterweight matters for product tone. **A third of the population lacked enough calories for an active life in 2024** ([Food Systems Dashboard](https://www.foodsystemsdashboard.org/countries/mmr/fsci/printable)). Calorie-cutting is an urban middle-class proposition, not a national one.

## The buyer is an urban woman of 30–55, or the family cooking for a diabetic

No market study profiles Myanmar's diet or fitness consumers, so the segments come from where the disease burden, the spending signals and the digital reach overlap. Three overlap strongly.

The first is **urban women of roughly 30–55 who care about weight and appearance**. They carry the highest overweight and prediabetes rates (above). They already spend on health. The US Commerce Department describes a Yangon- and Mandalay-centred supplement market in which **"beauty-from-within" products are especially strong among middle-aged women**. It is sold heavily through Facebook and Telegram, to buyers who are price-sensitive and prefer smaller, cheaper packs ([trade.gov, Sep 2026](https://www.trade.gov/market-intelligence/burma-nutritional-supplements)). Burmese-language weight-loss vlogs, such as diaries of losing 15 lb, show the motivation exists in the language ([YouTube example](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ez02wc8Zlbs)). No survey measures weight-loss intent directly, so this is a well-supported hypothesis, not a measured fact.

The second is **adults with diagnosed diabetes, prediabetes or hypertension, and the family members who shop and cook for them**. Yangon's diabetes awareness rose from 44% to 69% between 2004 and 2014, but the share with controlled diabetes did not improve ([Aung et al., IJERPH 2019](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6765960/)). Diagnosed patients know they have a problem and lack a tool to act on it. We infer that the payer is often an adult child in Yangon rather than the older patient. No source covers who pays, and this is a priority interview question.

The third is the **Burmese diaspora, led by Thailand**, a later but richer segment. About **4.6 million Myanmar people lived in Thailand by November 2025** ([Irrawaddy, citing IOM](https://www.irrawaddy.com/news/burma/nearly-700000-myanmar-nationals-enter-thailand-in-jan-apr-iom.html)). Most work low-wage jobs, but Bangkok's 400 THB daily minimum ([Khaosod English](https://www.khaosodenglish.com/news/business/2025/07/01/bangkok-minimum-wage-rises-to-400-baht-benefiting-700000-workers/)) is several times Myanmar's. Myanmar migrant workers in Chiang Mai had **27.8% hypertension**, and being overweight raised the odds about sixfold ([Aung et al., IJERPH 2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8955730/)). Japan (160,000–183,000 people) and Singapore (about 200,000 in 2017) are smaller pockets that can pay global prices ([Wikipedia: Burmese in Japan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burmese_people_in_Japan); [RFA](https://rfa.org/english/myanmar/2025/02/04/myanmar-nationals-get-singapore-passports/)). All of them eat Burmese food that Thai or English databases handle poorly.

Two segments look weaker than they might seem. **Gym-goers** pay well, at roughly 40,500–113,820 Ks a month for a Yangon gym ([CityCost](https://citycost.org/yangon/); [Expatistan](https://expatistan.com/price/gym/yangon)), but they are a small high-income group with no demographic data behind them. **Students and young men** are reachable on TikTok but sit closest to the price ceiling, and no data link them to weight concern.

How these users find health information shapes everything. **Facebook is the main health channel, carrying good information and misinformation side by side.** A single Myanmar nutrition-education page reached about **2.6 million people**, mostly organically ([Proceedings of the Nutrition Society, 2024](https://resolve.cambridge.org/core/journals/proceedings-of-the-nutrition-society/article/use-of-social-media-for-promoting-nutrition-in-a-conflictaffected-country-facebook-insights/C923EF9671FB3E7F6E5050C8D2D374F8)). Research in northern Myanmar found **limited Burmese-language health content and low trust in official sources** ([ERIA 2020](https://www.eria.org/uploads/media/Books/2020-E-commerce-Connectivity-in-ASEAN/18_Chapter-14-Connectivity-and-the-Healthcare-Market-in-Myanmar.pdf)). Sar AI will probably have to borrow trust from doctors and dietitians who already have a following there. One technical detail of the language matters too. In 2019 an estimated **85–90% of phones used the Zawgyi font encoding**, and 10–15% of older phones could not convert ([Inquirer/AFP](https://technology.inquirer.net/?p=90985); [Egypt Independent/AFP](https://www.egyptindependent.com/code-war-myanmars-final-digital-battle-ends/)). No current figure exists, but text input on cheap old phones should detect and convert Zawgyi.

## A connected but cash-poor, censored market rewards offline, kyat-priced design

### Phones and internet are mass-market, and Android dominates

**39.8 million people (72.5%) used the internet in October 2025**, on 62.5 million cellular connections, 96.8% of them 3G/4G-capable ([DataReportal 2026](https://datareportal.com/reports/digital-2026-myanmar)). **Android carries 80–84% of mobile web traffic** (StatCounter, June–July 2026), so Android-first covers most users. iOS, at 16–20%, is a larger, wealthier minority than the stereotype suggests ([StatCounter](https://gs.statcounter.com/os-market-share/mobile/myanmar)). The reach channels have shifted. **TikTok now reaches 21.0 million adults against Facebook's 13.7 million.** TikTok's audience is about 61% male, while Facebook's is roughly 50/50 (same DataReportal source), so Facebook remains the better way to reach women despite being blocked. Data is cheap enough. MPT sells a 30-day 1.2 GB pack for 2,699 Ks ([PhoneTravelWiz](https://www.phonetravelwiz.com/buying-a-sim-card-in-myanmar-guide)), and operators were competing with large data giveaways in late 2025 ([VnEconomy](https://vneconomy.vn/viettel-va-thi-truong-myanmar-bay-gio-canh-tranh-moi-khoc-liet.htm)). No 2025–26 data were found on smartphone brands, RAM or handset prices.

### Shutdowns, VPN bans and surveillance make offline-first mandatory

Freedom House scored Myanmar **9/100 in 2025**, among the world's worst ([Freedom House](https://freedomhouse.org/index%2Ephp/country/myanmar/freedom-net/2025)). There were **105 shutdowns across 73 townships in 2025**, mostly in conflict regions such as Sagaing, Magway, Tanintharyi and Ayeyarwady. Nearly half extended mobile-service cuts, so voice and SMS went down too ([Myanmar Internet Project](https://www.myanmarinternet.info/post/yearly_report_2025-1)). Urban Yangon and Mandalay mostly stay connected, but power does not. **Yangon residents in some townships report about 2 hours of grid power a day in October 2026** ([Irrawaddy](https://www.irrawaddy.com/news/burma/life-without-power-spells-daily-misery-for-yangons-residents.html)). That brings phone-charging problems and diesel-dependent cell towers. The Cybersecurity Law, in force since 30 July 2025, **criminalises unregistered VPN services and requires providers to keep user data for 3 years**. Phone checks for VPNs followed, and 197 people were arrested for social-media activity in 2025 (same source; [Rahmat Lim](https://rahmatlim.com/publication/articles/31053/cybersecurity-law-in-force-30-july-2025)). A broad VPN block was reported in November 2025 ([Internet in Myanmar](https://www.internetinmyanmar.com/digest/2025-11-14-myanmars-junta-intensifies-internet-crackdown-with-vpn-block/)). Facebook has been restricted since the coup ([Proc. Nutr. Soc. 2024](https://resolve.cambridge.org/core/journals/proceedings-of-the-nutrition-society/article/use-of-social-media-for-promoting-nutrition-in-a-conflictaffected-country-facebook-insights/C923EF9671FB3E7F6E5050C8D2D374F8)), yet its ad reach still grew 7.9%, which implies widespread VPN use. No source reports Google Play being blocked, but none confirms its status either.

### Wallets work; Google Play billing mostly does not

**KBZPay claims about 22 million users (19 million active at end-2024)**, and its transactions grew from 2.1 billion in 2024 to over 3.8 billion in 2025 ([Huawei case study](https://e.huawei.com/my/case-studies/industries/finance/kbzpay); [FinanceAsia](https://www.financeasia.com/article/winner-spotlight-2025-kbz-bank/503374)). Both figures come from promotional sources. Wave Money has historically held about 80% of mobile financial services ([Fintech Times](https://thefintechtimes.com/?p=133703)), and it offers a "Pay with Wave" online merchant API ([Wave Money](https://wavemoney.com.mm/partner/pay-with-wave)). Google Play lists Myanmar for paid apps and subscriptions in MMK ([Play Console Help](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/138294)). Its payment-methods page, however, offers Myanmar **only international credit and debit cards**, with no carrier billing, gift cards or wallets ([Google Play Help](https://support.google.com/googleplay/answer/2651410?hl=en)). That confirms the product plan's wallet-first choice. It also opens a policy question we could not resolve: whether selling in-app passes through KBZPay or WavePay is allowed under Play's payments policy for Myanmar. One weak signal on auto-renewal: MPT discounts its auto-renew data pack, 499 Ks against 599 Ks ([MPT](https://mpt.com.mm/en/mpt-introduces-mahar-data-pack-enjoy-favorite-myanmar-movies-and-series-anytime-en/)). That suggests customers must be paid to accept recurring billing, which supports prepaid passes.

### The economy is stabilising at a low level, under continued military rule

The World Bank estimates **GDP shrank about 2% in FY2025/26**, forecasts 2% growth for FY2026/27, and puts **poverty at 29.9%**. Inflation **peaked at 24.6% in April 2026** after the Hormuz fuel shock ([Myanmar Now/AFP, citing the World Bank](https://myanmar-now.org/en/news/myanmar-inflation-hits-25-on-us-iran-fuel-shock-world-bank/)). The kyat has three prices: the CBM reference rate of about 2,100 per dollar ([CBM](https://forex.cbm.gov.mm/index.php/fxrate)), a CBM market trading rate around 3,660–3,777, and a street rate near 4,600–4,700 in October 2026 ([MoeMaKa](https://moemaka.net/eng/2026/10/myanmar-kyat-depreciates-amid-growing-instability-raising-concerns-over-rising-consumer-prices/)). Politically, junta-run elections were followed by **Min Aung Hlaing's election as president on 3 April 2026**, so the military still governs ([NST](https://www.nst.com.my/amp/world/region/2026/04/1410016/myanmar-junta-chief-elected-president-pro-military-mps)). US sanctions cover the Ministry of Defense and the state banks MFTB and MICB ([US Treasury](https://home.treasury.gov/news/press-releases/jy1555)). Private wallets such as KBZPay and WavePay were not found on sanctions lists, though this research did not check them against the SDN list. No standalone personal-data or health-data protection law was found.

## No global app speaks Burmese; Binfo is the one rival to watch

The global market is consolidating around photo AI, and it is cheap in dollars. **MyFitnessPal bought Cal AI in March 2026**, after Cal AI reached more than 15 million downloads and over $30 million in annual revenue with a 7-person team ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/03/02/myfitnesspal-has-acquired-cal-ai-the-viral-calorie-app-built-by-teens)). Photo logging is now table stakes, not a moat. Pricing tracks that commoditisation, though most figures come from third-party and competitor blogs and Cal AI A/B-tests its paywall heavily.

| App | US price (2026, third-party sources) | Burmese UI | Myanmar food fit |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cal AI | ~$29.99/yr (~$2.50/mo) ([eesel.ai](https://www.eesel.ai/blog/cal-ai-pricing)) | None found | Generic photo estimates |
| MyFitnessPal | $19.99/mo or $79.99/yr ([FitBudd](https://www.fitbudd.com/post/myfitnesspal-app-cost)) | None found | Crowdsourced; inconsistent for SE Asian dishes |
| Lose It!, Yazio, Lifesum, Foodvisor | ~$24–84/yr ([CalorieTrackerLab](https://calorietrackerlab.com/articles/calorie-tracker-pricing-guide-2026/); [TheBiteTracker](https://thebitetracker.com/field-notes/cheapest-calorie-tracking-apps)) | None found | Not found |
| FatSecret | Freemium | Added Thai and Vietnamese, not Burmese ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/calorie-counter-by-fatsecret/id347184248)) | Deep Thai coverage, little Burmese |
| Binfo (Yangon) | Not published | Yes | Claims "10,000+ verified Myanmar food data"; no photo AI found ([binfo.app](https://binfo.app/)) |

Global apps fail Myanmar users in three ways. They have no Burmese interface. They rely on crowdsourced or generic entries, so MyFitnessPal has five "pad thai" entries ranging from 300 to 800 kcal ([Nutrola, a competitor's blog](https://nutrola.app/en/blog/best-calorie-tracking-app-for-thai-vietnamese-filipino-food-2026)). And they sell store-billed dollar subscriptions that most Myanmar users cannot pay for. Burmese dishes appear only on scattered web pages, such as Foodakl's 65 Burmese dishes ([Foodakl](https://foodakl.com/kitchens/burmese/)). No source positively confirms that every global app lacks Burmese, but none was found offering it.

**Binfo is the direct competitor.** Earthlike Co., Ltd. in Yangon makes it, and its Play listing calls it the first calorie tracker built for Myanmar. It offers calorie and macro calculators, a serving guide and progress tracking. Its site mentions "soft launch events", which suggests an early stage ([binfo.app](https://binfo.app/); [Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.earthlike.binfo&hl=en)). Its downloads, pricing, payment rails and any AI features are unverified. A hobbyist's open-source, offline Burmese calorie app on GitHub signals felt demand rather than competition ([GitHub](https://github.com/SHO101234/zixora-calories)). The indirect alternatives are telehealth apps, Facebook and YouTube creators, and dietitians. **MyanCare (700+ doctors)**, OnDoctor and Pun Hlaing's HEAL cover general consultations and medicine delivery, not food logging ([MyanCare](https://myancare.org/); [OnDoctor](https://ondoctor.com.mm/); [HEAL](https://punhlainghospitals.com/heal-app/)). Online consultations with Myanmar doctors are mostly listed at about **$25 for 10–30 minutes** ([Dr. Galen](https://drgalen.org/myanmar-doctors)), roughly ten months of Sar AI at the official rate. We found no data on dietitian fees or on named Burmese weight-loss Facebook groups.

**Sar AI's data moat is real but must be built.** No public, downloadable Myanmar national food composition table was found. The ASEAN database at Mahidol was last updated in 2014 and does not clearly include Myanmar ([INMU](https://inmu.mahidol.ac.th/aseanfoods/composition_data.html)). The practical stack has four parts. Use the **FAO Myanmar Food Atlas (2022)**, a photo-based portion guide that maps naturally onto plates of rice ([FAO](https://openknowledge.fao.org/server/api/core/bitstreams/7b1ed1b8-4339-4bdd-ab55-8e6e3d5e2a08/content)). Use ASEAN and USDA tables for ingredients. Calculate composite dishes recipe by recipe. And ask the Department of Medical Research in Yangon, the INFOODS country coordinator ([FAO INFOODS](https://www.fao.org/infoods/infoods/regional-data-centres/aseanfoods/en/)). The licence for commercial reuse of the Atlas was not checked.

Thailand is the opposite of Myanmar: crowded. It has Thai-language trackers such as CalCounter, CalTrack and a "Calorie Diary" with AI food scan ([Google Play: CalCounter](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.cpmatch.calcounter&hl=en); [Calorie Diary](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.dimo.util.caloriediary)), plus university Thai-dish recognition projects. Its rich markets also have working payment rails, in **PromptPay (about 56 million users) and TrueMoney (over 20 million)** ([Tazapay](https://tazapay.com/en-sg/blog/local-payment-methods-in-thailand-how-promptpay-works-in-an-international-payment-gateway); [TransFi](https://www.transfi.com/pt/payment-methods/online-payment-processors-in-thailand)). For Thailand, the opening is Burmese migrants, not Thai nationals.

## Recommendations: lead with women and rice, price small and prepaid

### Primary segment and positioning

**Target urban women aged roughly 30–55 in Yangon and Mandalay first**, with "managing blood sugar for yourself or a parent" as the second message in the same funnel. This group combines the highest measured risk, the strongest spending signal and the best-reached audience (Facebook skews to women, TikTok to men). It also has Yangon's higher diagnosis awareness. Defer students, gym-goers and Thai nationals. Treat Burmese migrants in Thailand as the first expansion, ahead of Thai users.

Position Sar AI as **the tracker that speaks Burmese and knows a plate of rice**. It shows you, in plates and spoons, where your rice, oil, sugar and salt go, and keeps working when the power and the internet don't. That puts the local wedge (language, rice units, shared meals, tea-shop food, offline) ahead of photo AI, which Cal AI has commoditised. Frame it as health, not dieting. That suits the prediabetes story, keeps the app away from the slimming-product culture on Facebook, and avoids encouraging calorie-cutting in a country where a third of people are undernourished. Be openly honest that the numbers are estimates. In a market with low trust in official sources and banned products still on sale ([DMG](https://dmediag.com/news/fda-checfood.html)), "estimate, checked against the FAO Food Atlas" can win more trust than false precision. Borrow credibility through partnerships. Burmese doctors and dietitians with Facebook and YouTube followings, telehealth apps such as MyanCare and OnDoctor, and the Myanmar Diabetes Association ([MDA](https://myanmardiabetes.org.mm/about-us/)) could all refer patients, which beats competing with them.

### Pricing sanity check: 4,900 Ks is defensible, not cheap

| Benchmark | Value | 4,900 Ks pass equals |
|---|---|---|
| Official rate (2,100/USD) ([CBM](https://forex.cbm.gov.mm/index.php/fxrate)) | $2.33 | About Cal AI's annual-plan monthly price |
| Street rate (~4,650/USD) ([MoeMaKa](https://moemaka.net/eng/2026/10/myanmar-kyat-depreciates-amid-growing-instability-raising-concerns-over-rising-consumer-prices/)) | $1.05 | Less than half of Cal AI |
| Minimum wage, 7,800 Ks/day ([Tilleke & Gibbins](https://www.tilleke.com/insights/myanmar-sets-new-minimum-wage)) | ~202,800 Ks/month (26 days, our assumption) | 63% of a day's wage, 2.4% of a month |
| Yangon civil engineer, ~$387/month ([Paylab](https://www.paylab.com/mm/salaries-in-country?lang=en)) | ~0.8–1.8M Ks, depending on the rate used | 0.3–0.6% of salary |
| MPT 30-day 1.2 GB data pack ([PhoneTravelWiz](https://www.phonetravelwiz.com/buying-a-sim-card-in-myanmar-guide)) | 2,699 Ks | 1.8× the user's main digital spend |
| Yangon gym ([CityCost](https://citycost.org/yangon/)) | ~40,500 Ks/month | About an eighth |
| Online doctor consult ([Dr. Galen](https://drgalen.org/myanmar-doctors)) | ~$25 | About a tenth |

The verdict is that **4,900 Ks a month is the right ballpark for salaried urban households and families managing diabetes**, and too high for minimum-wage earners and students. For them, the real anchor is the data pack, and the pass costs nearly twice as much. We recommend four adjustments, all untested hypotheses for interviews and A/B tests. First, add a **low-commitment entry point**, a 7-day pass or a scan top-up pack. That matches the small-pack buying habit seen in supplements ([trade.gov](https://www.trade.gov/market-intelligence/burma-nutritional-supplements)) and the prepaid culture. Second, **deepen the 3-month discount**. At 12,900 Ks it works out to 4,300 Ks a month, only 12% off, which is weak motivation to commit for longer. Third, **plan repricing every 6–12 months**. With inflation at 21–25%, a fixed 4,900 Ks loses about a fifth to a quarter of its real value within a year ([World Bank via Myanmar Now](https://myanmar-now.org/en/news/myanmar-inflation-hits-25-on-us-iran-fuel-shock-world-bank/)). Fourth, **set diaspora prices in local currency** (THB, SGD, JPY), well above the kyat price.

Set revenue expectations soberly. Freemium apps have a **median day-35 conversion of about 2.1%** ([Airbridge, citing RevenueCat](https://www.airbridge.io/blog/hard-vs-soft-paywalls)). Health and fitness download-to-paid medians are about 3.8–4.2% ([Adapty](https://adapty.io/state-of-in-app-subscriptions/)), and Android converts at roughly half the iOS rate ([Adapty blog](https://adapty.io/blog/freemium-to-premium-conversion-techniques)). A generous free tier (3 photo scans a day, unlimited voice and text) in a low-income Android market argues for planning on **1–2%**. As an illustration (our arithmetic): 100,000 monthly active users at 1.5% conversion gives 1,500 payers and about 7.35 million Ks a month. That is roughly $3,500 at the official rate, or $1,600 at the street rate. Myanmar alone funds a lean team, not a venture-scale business. The diaspora and Thailand are where the upside sits.

| Google Play payments policy | Selling passes via KBZPay/WavePay inside a Play-distributed app may breach Play's billing rules. Play offers Myanmar only card payments ([Google Play Help](https://support.google.com/googleplay/answer/2651410?hl=en)) | Get a policy or legal read before launch; consider web-based purchase, APK distribution or Play Billing as a parallel option |
| Daily-logging habit | No evidence that Myanmar users will log meals daily; shared meals make logging harder | Voice and photo logging, one-tap "same as yesterday", shared-dish splitting; test in interviews |
| Connectivity, power and OTP | 105 shutdowns in 2025 also cut SMS; Yangon has hours of power a day | Offline-first (already planned), offline grace period for passes, an alternative to SMS OTP such as Google sign-in |
| Data and surveillance law | 3-year data retention and state access under the Cybersecurity Law; health data is sensitive | Keep health data on the device, collect little on the server, get local legal advice on registration |
| Acquisition channel fragility | Facebook is blocked, VPNs are criminalised, and the Nov 2025 VPN block makes Facebook ads less reliable | Mix Facebook with TikTok, YouTube creators, telehealth partners and word of mouth |
| Kyat and inflation | The real price erodes 20–25% a year; three exchange rates confuse store pricing | Price in kyat, reprice often, use local-currency diaspora tiers |
| Nutrition accuracy | No public national composition table; a wrong number destroys trust | Label figures as estimates, use the FAO Atlas, publish the method, add dietitian review |
| Binfo or a well-funded entrant | Binfo already claims a verified Myanmar database | Install and benchmark Binfo now; compete on photo, voice, rice units and offline |
| Sanctions exposure | MFTB and MICB are sanctioned; payment chains need screening | Screen KBZ and Wave and their owners against OFAC, EU and UK lists before contracting |
| Zawgyi input | Older cheap phones may still type Zawgyi | Detect and convert Zawgyi in search and text input |

### Questions to validate in user interviews

Interview about 15–25 people across the three segments: Yangon/Mandalay women aged 30–55, diabetic patients and their carers, and Burmese workers in Thailand. Ask about real behaviour ("last time you…") rather than opinions. Then pair the interviews with a small pricing test.

| Hypothesis to test | Questions to ask |
|---|---|
| Weight and blood sugar are felt problems | What worries you most about your health or your family's? Have you tried to lose weight or cut rice? What did you do, and what happened? |
| Who pays for diabetes care | Who in your family has diabetes or high blood pressure? Who buys their food and medicine, and who decides what to spend? |
| Plates are the natural unit | How many plates of rice did you eat yesterday? Show me your plate. Would you describe it in plates, cups or spoons? |
| Shared meals can be logged | At your last family meal with shared curries, how would you say how much you ate? Would you log for the whole family or only yourself? |
| Tea shop and outside food matter | What did you eat outside the home this week? How often do you drink tea-shop tea or 3-in-1 mix? |
| Voice and photo beat typing | Do you send voice messages on Viber or Messenger? Would you rather speak, photograph or type what you ate? Do you type in Zawgyi or Unicode? |
| Trust comes from doctors and Facebook | Where do you get diet advice now? Whose advice do you trust? Would a doctor's or dietitian's recommendation make you try an app? |
| 4,900 Ks feels fair | What do you pay each month for data, apps or supplements? At what price would a monthly health app feel too expensive? At what price would it feel suspiciously cheap? Would you buy a 7-day pass first? |
| Prepaid beats auto-renew | Do you use auto-renew for data packs? How would you feel about a payment that renews automatically through KBZPay? |
| Daily logging is sustainable | Have you ever tracked anything daily, such as weight, money or blood sugar? What made you stop? |
| Outages change behaviour | How many hours of power and internet do you get each day? Do you use a VPN? Has an app ever stopped working for you because of this? |
| Diaspora fit (Thailand) | What food do you eat in Thailand, and is it mostly Burmese? How do you pay for apps there: PromptPay, TrueMoney, cards or family wallets in Myanmar? |

## Conclusion

The research changes where Sar AI's edge comes from. Photo AI looked like the headline feature, but Cal AI's sale to MyFitnessPal shows photo calorie estimates are now a commodity sold at $30 a year. What no global player can easily copy is a Burmese-speaking app that understands a shared table of rice and curries and runs offline through power cuts. It also sells a prepaid pass through the wallet people already use. The disease data make this a health product, not a vanity diet app. Diet-linked disease is widespread in towns, mostly undiagnosed, and harder to treat since 2021. That points the brand toward family health and blood-sugar management for the women who usually shop and cook for the household.

The commercial reality is just as clear. Myanmar can support a lean, profitable niche, but not a large business. The constraints are poverty, inflation, a censored internet and a Google Play that cannot take local money. The fastest way to lose is to pour effort into perfect pricing before confirming two cheaper facts: that people will log daily, and that the payment flow is allowed on Play. Settle those two, benchmark Binfo, and treat Burmese migrants in Thailand as the bridge to the higher-income market.
